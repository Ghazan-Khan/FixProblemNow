## Measure at the device, not at the router

A speed test on a phone next to the TV is a rough guide, but the device’s own connection matters more. If the device has a network status screen, use it. Otherwise, run a speed test on the same Wi-Fi in the same position.

Then compare with what the stream needs. If the connection is close to the requirement with no headroom, a small dip will cause buffering. Other devices, background updates, and video calls reduce the available bandwidth.

## Improve the link

### 1. Move the device or router

Streaming sticks are often hidden behind a TV, which blocks Wi-Fi. Use the manufacturer’s HDMI extender to move the stick away from the TV, or move the router higher and more central. Even a small change can improve stability.

### 2. Use Ethernet if possible

If the device has a LAN port, use a cable. If it is a stick, use the manufacturer’s Ethernet adapter if supported. A wired link removes Wi-Fi interference and is the most reliable test of whether Wi-Fi is the bottleneck.

### 3. Reduce competing traffic

Pause cloud backups, game downloads, and large uploads while testing. Check the router’s device list for unknown devices. If someone else is streaming 4K in another room, the available bandwidth for your device is lower.

### 4. Restart the router and device

Power-cycle the router, wait for it to stabilise, then restart the streaming device. Clear the app’s cache if it has one. A device that has been sleeping for weeks can accumulate stale network state.

## Check the app and service

### 5. Test another service

If one app buffers and another streams fine, the problem is likely that app or its content path. Update the app, clear its cache, and try a different title. Check the service’s status page for an outage.

### 6. Lower the quality as a test

Change the streaming quality from 4K to HD, or from HD to SD. If the buffering stops, bandwidth is the constraint. This is a diagnostic, not necessarily the permanent fix.

### 7. Check device heat and storage

Feel the device. A hot stick or box may throttle. Keep it ventilated, remove anything covering it, and check free storage. Unused apps and downloads can fill a small device.

### 8. Update the device firmware

Install the latest system update. If the device cannot complete an update because it keeps stalling, use Ethernet and try again.

## If buffering continues

- **Check the internet plan** and whether the provider is throttling or has an outage.
- **Try a different HDMI port and cable** if the picture also glitches; a bad cable can cause more than buffering.
- **Test the device on another network**, such as a phone hotspot, to separate the home network from the device.
- **Contact the streaming service** with the title, time, device, and error details.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not install “streaming booster” apps or change DNS settings you do not understand in an attempt to fix buffering. Bandwidth and stability are the main factors, and unknown apps can expose your accounts.

## Confirm the fix

Play the same title at the same quality for at least 15 minutes. Watch for resolution drops, not just full stops. Test at the time of day when the problem used to occur, and confirm that other devices on the network still work normally.
