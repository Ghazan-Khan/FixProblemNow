## Prepare both sides

### 1. Charge the accessory

Charge it fully, or put earbuds in a charged case. A device can power on with a weak battery but fail to pair or disconnect immediately. Use the cable and charger the manufacturer specifies.

### 2. Put the accessory into pairing mode

Follow the manual. Usually you hold the power or pairing button until a light flashes in a specific pattern. For earbuds, opening the case with both buds inside may be enough. Pairing mode often times out, so do this just before searching.

### 3. Disconnect it from other devices

Headphones and speakers often stay connected to the last phone or tablet. Turn Bluetooth off on the other device, or disconnect the accessory in its Bluetooth settings. A device that is already connected may not advertise for a new pairing.

## Pair from the phone or computer

### 4. Remove the old pairing

On the phone or computer, find the accessory in the Bluetooth list and choose Forget or Remove. Then repeat the removal on the accessory if it has a reset or clear-pairing function.

### 5. Restart Bluetooth

Turn Bluetooth off, wait a few seconds, and turn it on again. On a computer, you can also restart the Bluetooth service or the computer itself. Then search for devices and pair within a metre.

### 6. Check the driver and service on a computer

On Windows, open Device Manager and look for the Bluetooth adapter. Update the driver from the computer manufacturer’s support page. Check Services for the Bluetooth support service and restart it if needed. On macOS, check the Bluetooth menu and reset the Bluetooth module only as Apple documents.

### 7. Reduce interference and distance

Move away from USB 3.0 drives, hubs, microwaves, and crowded Wi-Fi equipment. Keep the accessory and the computer in the same room during pairing. Walls and bodies reduce range, especially for small earbuds.

## If pairing still fails

- **Test the accessory with another phone or computer.** If it pairs there, the original device’s Bluetooth adapter or driver is the problem.
- **Reset the accessory** using the manufacturer’s procedure. This clears old pairings and can fix a stuck radio.
- **Try a USB Bluetooth adapter** on a desktop or laptop with a failing internal adapter.
- **Update the accessory firmware** through its companion app if one exists.
- **Contact the manufacturer** if the accessory will not pair with any device after a reset.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not charge a swollen battery-powered accessory, and do not use a damaged charging cable. Stop using it if it becomes very hot or smells burning.

## Confirm the fix

Play audio or use the mouse or keyboard for at least 10 minutes, then walk to the edge of normal range and back. Disconnect and reconnect once to confirm the pairing is saved. If it drops only when a USB 3.0 device is active, treat that as interference rather than a pairing fault.
