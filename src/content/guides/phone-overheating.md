## Work out whether the heat is expected

A phone produces heat whenever it does work. The useful question is not “is it warm?” but “is the heat proportional to what I am doing, and does it go away?”

| Situation | Usually normal | Worth checking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gaming or video call | Warm case, warm screen | Painful to hold, warning appears |
| Fast charging | Warm near the charging port | Too hot to touch, charging stops |
| Navigation in a car | Warm in sunlight | Hot at night with the screen off |
| Idle in a pocket | Not warm | Warm with no apps running |

If the phone is hot at idle, the cause is more likely a stuck process, a failing battery, or a charging fault than ordinary use.

## Cool it down safely

### 1. Stop the load and remove the case

Pause the game, end the video call, or stop navigation. Remove the case so heat can escape, and place the phone on a hard, shaded surface. Do not put it on a bed, cushion, or in a closed bag.

### 2. Disconnect the charger if it is hot

If the phone is hot while charging, unplug it and let it cool before charging again. Use the original or a certified charger and cable. A charger that buzzes, smells hot, or has damaged insulation should be replaced, not repaired.

### 3. Move away from direct sun and heat

A car dashboard, a windowsill, or a heater can push a phone above its safe temperature even when it is idle. Move it to a cool, ventilated place and wait for it to return to normal before using it heavily.

### 4. Find the app using the most power

Open the battery settings and look at usage by app. A navigation app left running in the background, a cloud backup, or a game that did not close can keep the processor busy. Force-stop the specific app, then check the temperature again.

On iPhone, use Settings > Battery. On Android, use Settings > Battery > Battery usage. Look for an app with unusually high usage and no matching activity.

### 5. Check the charger and cable

Fast charging generates more heat than slow charging. If the phone only overheats while charging, test with a different certified charger and cable. Avoid charging under a pillow, in a hot car, or while playing a demanding game.

### 6. Update the phone and apps

A software bug can cause a radio or processor to stay busy. Install system and app updates, then restart the phone. If the heat started after an update, check the manufacturer’s support site for a known issue.

## If the phone still overheats

- **Start in safe mode** on Android to see whether a third-party app is responsible. If the heat stops, remove recently installed apps one at a time.
- **Check battery health.** On iPhone, open Settings > Battery > Battery Health. On many Android phones, the manufacturer’s support app shows battery status. A badly degraded battery may need replacement.
- **Reset network settings** if the phone is hot while searching for a weak signal. A phone transmitting at maximum power in a poor coverage area can warm up.
- **Back up and consider a repair.** If the phone is hot at idle after a restart and an update, the battery or charging circuit may be faulty.

> [!WARNING]
> Never puncture, bend, or squeeze a battery. If the case is bulging or the screen is lifting, stop charging, keep the phone away from flammable material, and have it serviced. A swollen battery is a fire risk, not a software problem.

## Confirm the fix

Use the phone normally for a day and check the temperature during charging and during the task that used to cause heat. A phone that stays cool at idle and only warms mildly under load is behaving normally. If the heat returns with the same app, charger, or location, that is the variable to change next.
