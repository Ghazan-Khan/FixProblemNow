## Start with the power path

Power problems are easier to solve when you test one link at a time. There are four links between the wall and the laptop's mainboard: the outlet, the charger, the cable, and the charging port. Change only one thing at a time so you know what made a difference.

### 1. Verify the outlet and power strip

Unplug the laptop charger and plug a lamp or phone charger into the same outlet. If that device also fails, the outlet, switch, or power strip is the problem. Move the laptop charger directly to a wall outlet and try again.

Avoid daisy-chaining extension leads or using a travel adapter that feels loose. A charger that wobbles in the outlet can make contact intermittently, which looks exactly like a laptop that turns on only sometimes.

### 2. Inspect the charger and cable

Check the length of the cable for cuts, sharp bends, and chew marks. Look at the connector: a barrel plug should be straight, and a USB-C plug should sit fully seated without force. Try a different cable if the charger has a detachable one.

Use the charger that came with the laptop or one the manufacturer lists as compatible. Chargers from another brand may fit physically but fail to negotiate the right voltage. Do not use a charger that buzzes, smells hot, or has exposed wiring.

### 3. Look for a charging indicator

With the charger connected, check the laptop's charging LED, the screen, and any indicator on the charger itself. Behaviour varies widely:

- **No light at all:** the charger or port is not delivering power, or the indicator is disabled in software.
- **Amber or orange light:** the battery is charging but may be too low to start.
- **White or green light:** the battery is charged or the laptop is receiving power.
- **Blinking light:** the manufacturer's manual usually assigns a meaning, often a battery or power fault.

If the light appears only when you wiggle the cable, stop using that cable or port. Intermittent connections can damage the charging circuit.

### 4. Remove everything except power

Disconnect USB drives, external monitors, docks, memory cards, printers, and headphones. A faulty peripheral or a powered dock can prevent startup or confuse the power supply. Try the power button again with only the charger connected.

### 5. Perform a manufacturer-approved power reset

Many laptops store residual power that can interfere with startup. The procedure differs by model:

- Some models require holding the power button for 15 to 30 seconds while unplugged.
- Others have a small pinhole reset button.
- Some have a removable battery that the manufacturer instructs you to take out.

Find the exact procedure in your model's manual or support page. Do not guess, and do not open a sealed case just to disconnect the battery.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A power reset is not the same as a factory reset. It does not erase your files. If a guide asks you to enter recovery mode or reinstall the operating system, that is a different, more disruptive step.

## If the laptop shows no sign of power

When there are no lights, sounds, or fan movement after the outlet, charger, and reset checks, narrow it down with these observations.

- **Does the charging light change when you plug in?** If yes, the port and charger are probably working, and the fault may be in the power button or mainboard.
- **Does the laptop start without the battery?** On models with an easily removable battery, the manufacturer may document this test. Do not attempt it on a built-in battery.
- **Does another compatible charger change anything?** A second charger is the quickest way to rule out the adapter.
- **Is the port clean and undamaged?** Lint can block a USB-C connector. A bent or loose port needs professional repair.

If the answer to all of these is no, further attempts at home are unlikely to help. Record the model number, what you tested, and any light patterns for the repair technician.

## If the laptop powers on but the screen stays black

A laptop can be running with a dark display. Try these checks before treating it as a power failure:

1. Press the brightness-up key several times. A brightness setting of zero can look like a dead screen.
2. Connect an external monitor using the correct cable. If the external screen shows the desktop, the laptop is booting and the built-in display or its cable is the likely fault.
3. Use the display-toggle shortcut for your model, often a function key with a monitor icon.
4. Shine a torch at an angle across the screen. If you can faintly see the desktop, the backlight may have failed.
5. Hold the power button to shut down completely, wait ten seconds, and start again.

If the external monitor also shows nothing, the problem is more likely startup, memory, or graphics hardware rather than the screen itself. Our guide to a computer that turns on with a black screen covers those checks in more detail.

## If the laptop starts but shuts down immediately

A laptop that begins to boot and then loses power may be overheating, running on a failing battery, or hitting a startup error.

- Listen for the fan. If it runs at full speed and the case becomes hot quickly, shut down and let it cool.
- Check that the vents are not blocked by a bed, cushion, or dust. Use compressed air only according to the manufacturer's guidance.
- Remove the charger and see whether the laptop stays on with battery power. If it only fails while charging, the adapter or charging circuit may be involved.
- Disconnect all peripherals and try starting again.
- If the laptop beeps or shows a diagnostic code, look up that pattern in the manufacturer's manual rather than guessing.

Do not keep restarting a laptop that repeatedly overheats or shuts down. Repeated thermal shutdowns can worsen hardware damage.

## Reduce the chance of it happening again

Once the laptop is working, make the next failure easier to handle:

- Keep the charger and cable in a place where they will not be bent or crushed.
- Use a surge protector in areas with unstable power, and avoid cheap unregulated adapters.
- Do not store a laptop with a completely flat battery for long periods. Charge it to roughly half before storage.
- Keep the vents clear and clean dust from the exterior according to the manufacturer's instructions.
- Confirm that important files are backed up somewhere other than the laptop.

> [!TIP]
> If the laptop is under warranty, contact the manufacturer before opening it or using a third-party repair shop. Some warranties are affected by unauthorised disassembly.
