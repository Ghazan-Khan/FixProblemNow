## Start with the simple checks

### 1. Check mute and both volume controls

Press mute once and watch for an on-screen indicator. Raise the TV volume to a clearly audible level. Then check the source device’s own volume: a set-top box, console, or streaming stick can be muted independently of the TV.

### 2. Check whether the source is paused or silent

Play a different title or switch to a channel you know has sound. Some apps start muted or have their own volume slider, especially on mobile-cast sessions.

### 3. Confirm the TV is using the speakers you expect

Open the TV’s sound settings. If the output is set to HDMI ARC, optical, or Bluetooth, the TV speakers will be silent. Switch to TV speakers to test whether the TV can produce sound at all.

## Fix external audio

### 4. Check the soundbar, receiver, and input

Confirm the external device is powered on and set to the correct input. If the TV is connected by HDMI ARC/eARC, the soundbar must be on the ARC port on both ends. Try the soundbar with another source to prove it works.

### 5. Reseat or replace the cable

Unplug the HDMI or optical cable at both ends and reconnect it firmly. Try a different HDMI cable, especially if the current one is old or thin. For ARC, use the port labelled ARC or eARC, not just any HDMI port.

### 6. Match the audio format

If the TV or soundbar supports only stereo, set the source to PCM or stereo output. A Dolby or DTS stream sent to a device that cannot decode it can produce silence. Change one setting at a time and retest.

### 7. Check CEC and device control

HDMI-CEC can switch inputs and audio routing unexpectedly. If the TV keeps changing to an external speaker, check the CEC settings on both devices. Turning CEC off is a useful test, though it may disable one-remote control.

## Reset the audio path

### 8. Restart the TV and source

Turn off the TV and the source device, unplug the TV for a minute, then power both on. A full power cycle clears audio routing states that a standby cycle may not.

### 9. Update the TV and app

Install the latest TV firmware and app updates. A known audio bug may already be fixed. If the problem started after an update, check the manufacturer’s support notes.

### 10. Reset sound settings

Use the TV’s sound reset option rather than a full factory reset. This restores default audio output and format settings without removing apps.

## If there is still no sound

- **Test the TV’s own menu sounds.** If those are silent, the internal speakers or audio circuit may be faulty.
- **Connect headphones or an external speaker** to the TV’s audio output to see whether the TV is producing audio at all.
- **Try another source device** on the same input to rule out the source.
- **Contact the manufacturer** if no output works after a power cycle and sound reset.

> [!WARNING]
> Lower the volume before reconnecting speakers or headphones. A sudden full-volume output can damage hearing and speaker drivers.

## Confirm the fix

Test the TV menu, a live channel if available, and one streaming app. If you use a soundbar, confirm both remotes control volume as expected. Switch inputs and back to make sure the audio routing stays correct.
