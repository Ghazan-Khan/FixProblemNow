## Measure the drain before changing settings

“Fast” is not a measurement. Note the battery percentage when you stop using the phone at night and again when you wake up. Then note the screen-on time for a normal day from the battery screen.

A phone that loses 5% overnight is behaving normally. One that loses 25% while idle has a background process or a signal problem. One that loses charge quickly only while you use it may simply have a small battery or a demanding app.

## Reduce the biggest consumers

### 1. Check the battery usage list

- **iPhone:** Settings > Battery. Scroll to the app list and switch between 24 hours and 10 days.
- **Android:** Settings > Battery > Battery usage. Look for apps with high usage and long background time.

Do not assume the top app is misbehaving; a navigation app used for two hours legitimately uses power. Look for an app with high background usage that you barely opened.

### 2. Restrict background activity for non-essential apps

For apps that do not need to run constantly, limit background refresh or set them to restricted. Keep messaging, calls, and alarm apps unrestricted. Social, shopping, and news apps rarely need continuous background access.

### 3. Lower screen brightness and refresh rate

Set brightness to automatic and reduce the maximum if the screen is often at full brightness. On phones with a high refresh-rate display, try the standard rate for a day. Turn off always-on display if you do not rely on it.

### 4. Check signal and hotspot use

A weak mobile signal forces the phone to transmit at higher power. If you are in a poor-coverage area, use Wi-Fi calling or airplane mode with Wi-Fi. Turn off the hotspot when you are not using it, and disconnect devices that are idle.

### 5. Review location and sync settings

Set location access to “while using” for most apps, and turn off precise location where it is not needed. Check cloud photo sync and email fetch settings; constant uploads and frequent mail checks add up.

### 6. Install updates and restart

Install system and app updates, then restart the phone. If the drain started after an update, wait for a patch but check the manufacturer’s support site for a known issue. A restart clears stuck processes that survive normal app closing.

## If the drain continues

- **Test in safe mode** on Android. If the drain stops, a third-party app is responsible; remove recent apps one at a time.
- **Check battery health.** On iPhone, open Settings > Battery > Battery Health. On Android, use the manufacturer’s support app or service menu where available.
- **Calibrate your expectations.** Batteries age. A two-year-old phone with 82% health will not match a new one, even after every setting is optimised.
- **Back up and get a battery service** if the phone shuts down at a high percentage, swells, or remains hot at idle after a restart.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not puncture, bend, or freeze a battery, and do not charge a phone whose case is bulging. Move a swollen phone away from flammable material and have it serviced promptly.

## Confirm the fix

Repeat the overnight test and compare the screen-on time. If the drain improved, keep the change and revisit it after a week; some apps re-enable background activity after an update. If nothing changed, the battery itself or a system process is the next thing to check.
