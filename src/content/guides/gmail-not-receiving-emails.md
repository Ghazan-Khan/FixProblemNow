## Look everywhere before assuming mail is lost

### 1. Search all mail, including spam and trash

In Gmail search, use `in:anywhere from:sender@example.com` or `in:anywhere subject:"exact subject"`. Default search can exclude Spam and Trash, so this is the fastest way to find a message that was moved rather than lost.

Also check All Mail, Spam, and Trash manually. A message can be archived, labelled, or filtered without ever appearing in the inbox.

### 2. Check filters and blocked addresses

Open Settings > Filters and Blocked Addresses. Look for a filter that matches the sender, subject, or a word in the message, and check whether it archives, deletes, or applies a label. Check the blocked list for the sender’s address or domain.

A filter that deletes matching mail is easy to create and easy to forget. If you find one, edit or delete it, then ask the sender to resend.

### 3. Confirm storage is not full

Open your Google account storage settings and check the shared total for Gmail, Drive, and Photos. If it is full, new mail may be rejected. Empty Trash and Spam, remove large attachments, and review Drive and Photos files. Buying more storage is optional; freeing space is usually enough.

### 4. Send a test message

Send an email from a different account to the affected address. If it arrives, Gmail is receiving mail and the original sender or message is the likely problem. If it does not arrive, continue with the account-level checks.

### 5. Check forwarding and POP/IMAP

Open Settings > Forwarding and POP/IMAP. A forwarding rule can send new mail elsewhere, and a POP setting can remove messages from the inbox after download. Disable rules you did not create and check the forwarding address carefully.

### 6. Check the mail client, not just the app

Open Gmail in a browser. If the message is in the web inbox but not in a desktop or phone client, the client has a sync, filter, or account problem. Remove and re-add the account in that client, or use the Gmail app as a comparison.

### 7. Ask the sender for the bounce message

If a sender’s mail never arrives, ask them to check their sent folder and any delivery failure notification. A rejection message often names the reason, such as a full mailbox, a blocked address, or a policy rejection. This is more reliable than guessing.

## If mail is still missing

- **Check Gmail’s Spam and Trash retention.** Trash empties automatically after about 30 days; Spam after about 30 days. Recover a message before then if you find it.
- **Review account activity** for unfamiliar sign-ins. If someone else has access, change the password and enable two-step verification.
- **Try Gmail’s troubleshooting tool** in the Help Centre for missing messages.
- **Contact the sender’s mail administrator** if their server is rejecting mail to your address.

> [!WARNING]
> Google, Microsoft, and your bank will not ask for your password by email. If a “missing email” message asks you to sign in through a link, open the service directly instead. Report and delete the message.

## Confirm the fix

Send a test from two different providers, confirm it arrives on the web and in the app, and ask the original sender to resend. Check that the filters and blocked list no longer match their address. If the mail arrives but is labelled, that is a filter you can adjust rather than a delivery failure.
