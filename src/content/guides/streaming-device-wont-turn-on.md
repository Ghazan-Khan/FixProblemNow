## Prove the device has power

### 1. Check the light

Most streaming devices have a small status light. No light at all usually means no power or a failed device. A blinking light can indicate booting, pairing mode, or an error; check the manufacturer’s meaning for your model.

### 2. Use the original adapter and a wall outlet

Connect the device to a wall outlet with the adapter it came with. TV USB ports are convenient but often underpowered. If the device came with a USB cable and no adapter, use a reputable adapter rated for the device’s current requirement.

### 3. Check the cable and connector

Try a different USB cable if the device uses one, and make sure the connector is fully seated. A cable that charges a phone may not carry enough current for a streaming stick.

## Prove the display path

### 4. Select the correct input

Use the TV remote’s input/source button and cycle through the HDMI ports slowly. The correct port may not be the one you expect, especially after moving cables.

### 5. Try another HDMI port and cable

Move the device to a different HDMI port. For a stick, use the manufacturer’s HDMI extender if one was included; it can reduce strain and interference. For a box, try a different HDMI cable.

### 6. Power-cycle the TV and device

Unplug the TV and the streaming device for a minute, then power the TV on first and select the input, then power the device. This can clear an HDMI handshake problem.

## Recover the device

### 7. Pair or replace the remote

If the device has a light but does not respond, the remote may be unpaired or out of battery. Replace the batteries and follow the manufacturer’s pairing steps. Many devices also have a phone app remote that can prove the device is running.

### 8. Reset the device

Use the manufacturer’s reset procedure for your model. This removes accounts and settings, so note your logins. A reset is reasonable after a boot loop or a failed update, but not before checking power and HDMI.

### 9. Update firmware if it starts

If the device reaches the home screen, install any pending update and restart it. A failed update can cause a boot loop, and a completed update may fix it.

## If it still will not turn on

- **Try the device on another TV** to separate the TV port from the device.
- **Try another known-good power adapter** with the same voltage and adequate current.
- **Check for a recall or support notice** for your model.
- **Replace the device** if it will not power on with proven power and display paths.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not use a power adapter with a higher voltage than the device specifies, and do not open the casing. A damaged adapter or cable should be replaced, not repaired.

## Confirm the fix

Power the device on, reach the home screen, and play a few minutes of video. Switch the TV to another input and back to confirm the device is still running. If it restarts during playback, check ventilation and the power adapter before resetting again.
