## Check the simple parts first

Charging involves four parts: the wall outlet, the adapter, the cable, and the phone's port and battery. Replace one part at a time with something known to work. Randomly changing several parts makes it impossible to know which one failed.

### 1. Test the outlet

Plug a lamp or another charger into the same outlet. If the outlet is controlled by a wall switch, check the switch. Avoid testing with a power strip that has its own switch or breaker until you have confirmed the wall outlet works.

### 2. Use a known-good adapter and cable

Use the charger that came with the phone or a certified equivalent. Check the ratings printed on the adapter, especially for USB-C phones that expect Power Delivery. A charger with the right connector but the wrong protocol may charge very slowly or not at all.

Try a second cable even if the first looks fine. Cables fail internally near the connector, and the damage is often invisible. Borrow a cable that is currently charging another device successfully.

### 3. Inspect the charging port

With the phone powered off, shine a torch into the port. Look for:

- Compacted lint or dust at the back of the port.
- A bent pin or a cracked plastic tongue.
- Corrosion, green residue, or signs of liquid.
- A cable that does not click into place.

If you see loose debris, a dry wooden toothpick can gently lift it out. Keep the phone face down so debris falls away from the port. Do not scrape the contacts, use metal, or spray cleaner into the port.

> [!WARNING]
> Never use a paper clip, pin, or compressed air directly into a charging port. Metal can short the contacts, and high-pressure air can drive debris deeper or damage the port.

### 4. Try a different cable orientation and port

For USB-C, unplug the cable, rotate it, and reconnect. USB-C is reversible, but a worn connector may make contact in only one orientation. If your phone has more than one USB-C port, test the other one. On laptops, test a different USB port.

### 5. Force restart the phone

A frozen charging controller or system process can stop the charging indicator from updating. A force restart does not erase data.

- **iPhone with Face ID:** press and release volume up, press and release volume down, then hold the side button until the Apple logo appears.
- **iPhone with a Home button:** hold the side or top button and the Home button until the Apple logo appears.
- **Android:** hold the power button for about 10 to 20 seconds. Some models use power plus volume down. Check the manufacturer's instructions for your model.

After it restarts, connect the charger again and watch for the charging icon.

### 6. Charge while the phone is off

Power the phone off completely and connect the charger. If it charges while off but not while on, a background app or charging setting is involved. On Android, start in Safe Mode to see whether a third-party app is responsible. On iPhone, check for a recent iOS update and review battery settings.

### 7. Check battery protection settings

Modern phones deliberately manage charging:

- **iPhone:** Settings > Battery > Charging shows Optimized Battery Charging. A pause around 80 percent can be normal.
- **Android:** Settings > Battery may include Adaptive Charging, Battery Protect, or a charge limit.
- **Laptop-style limits:** Some phones and tablets let you cap charging at 80 or 85 percent.

Temporarily disabling a charge limit is reasonable for testing, but leaving it on can extend battery life. Do not disable it permanently unless you understand the trade-off.

### 8. Try wireless charging, if supported

A wireless charger bypasses the USB port. If the phone charges wirelessly but not by cable, the port is the likely fault. Remove thick cases and align the phone with the charging coil. Wireless charging is a diagnostic and a temporary workaround, not a repair.

## If the phone shows a charging icon but the percentage falls

This usually points to power use exceeding charge input or a battery that cannot hold charge.

- Close demanding apps and stop using the phone while it charges.
- Remove the case if the phone becomes warm.
- Use a higher-output certified charger if the phone supports fast charging.
- Check the battery health screen. iPhone shows Maximum Capacity under Battery Health; Android varies by manufacturer.
- Look for apps with unusually high background usage in battery settings.
- Test in Safe Mode on Android to rule out third-party apps.

If the percentage still falls while the phone is idle and connected to a known-good charger, the battery or charging circuit may need service.

## If the phone will not turn on at all

Leave it on a known-good charger for at least 30 minutes. A deeply discharged battery may need time before the screen responds. Then try a force restart. If there is still no display, no vibration, and no charging indicator, stop repeated attempts and arrange service.

Do not put a phone in rice. Rice does not reliably remove liquid and can introduce dust and starch into the port. If liquid exposure is suspected, power the phone off, do not charge it, and follow the manufacturer's guidance.

## Verify the fix

Charge the phone from below 30 percent to at least 50 percent using the cable and adapter that worked. Check that:

- The charging icon stays on without moving the cable.
- The percentage rises steadily.
- The phone does not become unusually hot.
- The connection survives a gentle cable wiggle.

If the charge stops when the cable moves, the port or cable is still loose. Replace the cable first, then have the port inspected.

## When to stop

Stop and seek qualified service if the battery or back cover is swollen, the port is bent or corroded, the phone gets hot enough to be uncomfortable, or charging fails after known-good equipment and a restart. Battery replacement and port repair require opening the device. Use an authorised repairer if the phone is under warranty.
